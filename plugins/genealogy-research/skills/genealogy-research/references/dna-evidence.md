# DNA evidence

Use this reference for autosomal, Y-DNA, mitochondrial DNA, X-DNA, chromosome-segment, match-cluster, unknown-parentage, adoption, or genetic-relationship questions. Also apply [privacy-and-ethics.md](privacy-and-ethics.md).

## Scope the question

Define the precise relationship or lineage question before interpreting results. Record:

- test type, company/platform, kit owner role, and consent scope;
- whether the data is a match list, total shared DNA, segment data, haplogroup, ethnicity estimate, raw file, or third-party prediction;
- known pedigree paths and documentary evidence;
- endogamy, pedigree collapse, multiple relationships, population context, and missing testers;
- platform version/date because estimates, labels, and matching algorithms change.

Do not request raw data when match summaries are sufficient. Do not copy living match names into shared artifacts; use stable private identifiers.

## Evidence discipline

- A centimorgan total usually supports a range of possible relationships, not one unique relationship.
- Ethnicity estimates are model-dependent population estimates and do not establish a genealogical relationship, citizenship, community membership, or personal identity.
- A triangulated segment supports shared inheritance but does not by itself identify the ancestral couple or rule out multiple paths.
- Y-DNA and mitochondrial DNA address specific direct lines; they do not represent the whole pedigree.
- Absence of a match can have several explanations, including inheritance variance, thresholding, test type, platform coverage, relationship distance, or a false documentary assumption.
- Trees attached to DNA matches are leads until their underlying records are evaluated.

Integrate genetic and documentary evidence. Build candidate hypotheses that predict both the observed DNA and the paper trail, then actively test alternatives.

## Analysis packet

For each material match or cluster, record:

| Field | Content |
|---|---|
| Private match ID | Stable pseudonymous identifier |
| Tester role | Relationship to the research subject |
| Test/platform/date | Include data version or access date |
| Shared DNA evidence | Total, segments, longest segment, or lineage result as available |
| Predicted ranges | Platform prediction plus other plausible relationships |
| Known pedigree path | With documentary source references |
| Shared matches/cluster | Evidence and limitations |
| Endogamy/multiple paths | Known or plausible impact |
| Hypotheses supported/challenged | Do not collapse to one answer prematurely |
| Next discriminating test | Additional tester, record, segment comparison, or lineage test |

Keep match identity, contact information, and sensitive conclusions in a private appendix separate from any shareable proof narrative.

## Testing plans

Recommend the least intrusive test that can distinguish the leading hypotheses. State who would be informative and why, but do not pressure, recruit, contact, or test anyone. A testing proposal should cover informed consent, possible unexpected results, data retention, platform sharing, law-enforcement matching settings where applicable, withdrawal limits, cost, and who can access results.

## Unknown parentage and sensitive findings

Use neutral hypothesis labels. Do not name a living biological parent from one match, one tree, or one predicted relationship. Seek multiple independent lines of documentary and genetic evidence, account for close relatives who produce similar patterns, and distinguish biological, legal, adoptive, social, and family identities.

Do not reveal a conclusion to a third party or contact a match. Present the user with a private evidence summary, uncertainty, plausible alternatives, and options for qualified support.

## Conclusion quality

A genetic conclusion should explain:

- why the selected test and testers were relevant;
- how data quality, relationship ranges, phasing, and population effects were handled;
- how genetic evidence correlates with documentary evidence;
- how competing relationships and multiple inheritance paths were tested;
- what privacy and consent constraints govern the result;
- what evidence could revise the conclusion.

Do not claim compliance with professional genetic-genealogy standards merely because DNA was included. Keep the conclusion provisional when verifiable data, tester roles, documentary correlation, or privacy permissions are incomplete.
