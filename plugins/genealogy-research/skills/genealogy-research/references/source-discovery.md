# Source discovery

Use this reference to design searches and identify current archives, catalogs, databases, and record coverage.

## Start with place and jurisdiction

Resolve the historical place before searching for a person. Determine civil, church, colonial, military, judicial, and archival jurisdictions for the relevant date. Account for boundary changes, renamed places, parish coverage, record transfer, language, script, calendars, and record loss.

Build a locality source map across likely record groups:

- civil registration and vital records;
- church, synagogue, mosque, or other community registers;
- census, population registers, household examinations, and tax lists;
- probate, guardianship, land, court, and notarial records;
- military, pension, draft, migration, naturalization, passport, and border records;
- directories, newspapers, obituaries, cemetery and funeral records;
- employment, school, institutional, guild, poor-law, and local-government records;
- manuscripts, family papers, oral histories, published genealogies, and local histories.

## Search strategy

- Search catalog metadata and finding aids, not only name indexes.
- Verify collection coverage by year, place, denomination, record type, and image availability.
- Use recorded-language variants, transliterations, phonetic variants, maiden names, patronymics, aliases, farm/house names, titles, and initials.
- Search associates, witnesses, sponsors, neighbors, employers, and collateral relatives when identity evidence is thin.
- Browse original images around an indexed entry; page context often reveals households, witnesses, headings, or systematic indexing errors.
- Treat user-contributed trees as leads and trace every useful assertion to its cited origin.

## Current-source rule

Websites, access policies, database coverage, and URLs change. When recommending or executing online research, browse current official repository, archive, or database documentation rather than relying on a static list. Prefer official national, regional, municipal, church, university, library, and archive sources; use aggregators as access paths or finding aids.

For each recommended source, state:

- holding institution or database;
- exact collection and coverage;
- why it can answer the question;
- access method, cost/login status, and image/index availability;
- search parameters and variants;
- citation fields to capture;
- fallback if access or coverage fails.

Do not bypass bot protection, paywalls, terms of service, robots rules, or account controls. If a human must log in, download an image, request a record, or visit an archive, prepare precise instructions and a capture checklist.
