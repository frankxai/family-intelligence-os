# Citations and outputs

Use when producing a citation, research plan, log, evidence ledger, proof summary, or handoff.

## Citation principle

A citation should identify the item actually consulted and let another researcher locate and evaluate it. Capture fields at discovery time. Never fabricate a missing field to make a citation look complete.

For a historical record, collect as applicable:

- creator, jurisdiction, church, archive, or record office;
- record set or series title;
- record type and named subject;
- event date and place;
- volume, book, certificate, entry, folio, page, image, film, box, or call number;
- repository or database;
- stable URL and access date;
- the underlying original record or microfilm reference;
- provenance of a user-held artifact.

Use a clear item-to-container-to-repository pattern. When a database hosts an image of an archival record, identify both the image/database access path and the underlying record. If only an index was consulted, say so.

Do not claim that a citation follows a proprietary style manual exactly unless its required fields and punctuation have been verified against that manual. Completeness and reproducibility matter more than decorative punctuation.

## Research plan

Use `assets/templates/research-plan.md`. Prioritize sources by expected information value and independence, not convenience alone. Include known coverage gaps, access constraints, success criteria, and stopping conditions.

## Research log

Use `assets/templates/research-log.md`. Record positive and negative results. One row or section should be understandable without hidden chat context.

## Evidence ledger

Use `assets/templates/evidence-ledger.md`. Give each source and claim a stable identifier. Keep source classification separate from information and evidence classification. Track shared provenance so duplicates do not inflate corroboration.

## Proof summary

Use `assets/templates/proof-summary.md` when evidence is straightforward and conflicts are limited. Use a fuller proof argument when the conclusion depends on multiple indirect items or substantial conflict resolution.

Every conclusion should state:

- the precise question;
- conclusion status;
- research scope and material limitations;
- evidence supporting and challenging the conclusion;
- analysis of source independence and informant quality;
- conflict resolution;
- remaining uncertainty and highest-value next test.

## Handoff packet

For substantial research, finish with a compact handoff containing:

1. What is established, supported, hypothesized, contradicted, and unresolved.
2. New or corrected citations.
3. Search log and meaningful negative findings.
4. Privacy, rights, and distribution restrictions.
5. Files created or changed, with originals preserved.
6. Next actions ordered by expected ability to change the conclusion.
