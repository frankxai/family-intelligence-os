# Privacy, ethics, and authority

Read this reference before DNA work, adoption or unknown-parentage research, processing information about living people, contacting anyone, publishing, or changing an external tree.

## Default posture

Follow the Coalition for Responsible AI in Genealogy themes of accuracy, disclosure, privacy, education, and compliance. Keep sensitive family data local when possible and disclose material AI assistance when preparing work for other people.

Treat these as sensitive unless the user establishes a narrower policy:

- living people and minors;
- recently deceased people whose records affect living relatives;
- dates of birth, addresses, contact information, identity numbers, medical or financial data;
- DNA files, match lists, segment data, ethnicity estimates, and inferred biological relationships;
- adoption, donor conception, non-parental events, criminal matters, institutionalization, and other potentially harmful findings;
- private correspondence, oral histories, and documents with unclear rights.
- culturally restricted, sacred, tribal, clan, community-held, or repatriation-sensitive knowledge.

## Data handling

- Use the minimum data needed for the question.
- Redact living-person identifiers before web searches, external models, or shared reports.
- Do not place real family data in repository fixtures, examples, logs, or screenshots.
- Keep source files unchanged. Record hashes or stable storage references when provenance matters.
- Distinguish private research rights from permission to share, publish, or process with AI.
- Comply with archive terms, database terms, copyright, access restrictions, and applicable privacy law.
- Respect community authority and culturally specific access protocols. Do not equate documentary descent, DNA similarity, or a database label with citizenship, membership, identity, or authority to disclose community knowledge.

## DNA and unexpected relationships

DNA is evidence to correlate with documentary evidence, not a self-interpreting verdict. Do not infer a named living parent or other sensitive relationship from ethnicity estimates or one match. State platform limitations, endogamy/pedigree-collapse concerns, relationship ranges, tester roles, and missing comparison data.

Do not:

- upload raw DNA or match data to another service without explicit authorization;
- encourage covert testing or impersonation;
- reveal an unexpected relationship to a third party;
- contact matches or relatives;
- treat a centimorgan total as a unique relationship;
- publish living-person DNA details.

Prepare a private, neutral findings brief and let the user decide whether and how to proceed. When emotional, legal, or safety consequences may be serious, recommend a qualified genetic genealogist, counselor, lawyer, or relevant professional without making the decision for the user.

For the evidence workflow itself, use [dna-evidence.md](dna-evidence.md).

## External actions

Read-only research on public sources is allowed when within the user's request. Get explicit authorization immediately before:

- buying a record or subscription;
- logging into an account or using supplied credentials;
- uploading a private file;
- editing or merging an online tree;
- messaging a repository, match, or relative;
- publishing or sharing a report;
- exporting living-person or DNA data.

Before an approved write, show the exact target and change, record the human confirmation, preserve the prior state, create an audit note, and state how to undo the action. Never make an agent's inference authoritative by accepting a claim or merging identities automatically.

## Reporting

Use neutral language. Separate documented facts, attributed family accounts, interpretations, and hypotheses. Avoid sensational framing. Include privacy and distribution markings such as `private`, `family_only`, `consent_required`, or `publication_cleared` when the destination supports them.

When oral history or private correspondence is used, record the contributor, consent scope, access restrictions, recording/transcription status, and whether the account may be quoted, summarized, shared with family, or published. A contributor's permission does not automatically cover living people discussed in the account.
