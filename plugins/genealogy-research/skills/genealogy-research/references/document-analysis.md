# Historical-document analysis

Use for photographs, scans, certificates, registers, manuscripts, letters, tables, marginal notes, or handwritten material.

## Preserve provenance

Before interpreting, record what is known about the artifact: owner/custodian, repository, collection, call number, volume, page/image, file name, URL, access date, capture method, and whether the image is original, derivative, cropped, enhanced, or AI-restored. Preserve the original file and work from a copy when enhancement is needed.

For any generated, reconstructed, colorized, or AI-modified historical image:

- label it visibly as modified or generated;
- cite the original and record the tool, date, and material edits;
- retain the unmodified original beside it;
- use the altered version as illustration only, never as evidence of identity, age, appearance, time, place, handwriting, damage, or an otherwise unseen detail.

## Separate four layers

1. **Observation:** document type, language/script, layout, stamps, seals, damage, pagination, and visible annotations.
2. **Diplomatic transcription:** reproduce text and meaningful line breaks as seen. Use `[illegible]`, `[?]`, and bracketed expansions; never silently repair names or dates.
3. **Translation:** retain the transcription and translate separately. Preserve ambiguous terms, titles, occupations, place names, and calendar expressions with notes.
4. **Interpretation:** extract people, events, relationships, places, dates, informants, witnesses, and candidate claims. Label inferred relationships as inferences.

Do not quote an index as if it were the image. If only an index is available, describe it as a derivative source and record that image verification remains pending.

## Reading discipline

- Inspect the entire page and neighboring entries for column meanings, ditto marks, headings, and scribal conventions.
- Compare uncertain letterforms with repeated words or names written by the same hand.
- Preserve the recorded spelling and store normalized names as separate variants.
- Identify the calendar and jurisdiction before converting dates. Record both original and normalized dates and explain the conversion.
- Do not infer ethnicity, religion, biological relationship, or legal status from a name alone.
- Preserve historically recorded racial, ethnic, disability, legitimacy, occupation, and status terms as quoted source language when material, but do not silently adopt them as neutral modern identity labels.
- When image quality is insufficient, state the limitation and request a higher-resolution, uncropped, color image when available.

## Extraction packet

Return:

- provenance and image-quality note;
- diplomatic transcription with uncertainty markers;
- translation when needed;
- structured entities and atomic claims;
- explicit vs inferred relationships;
- conflicts with existing evidence;
- citation fields captured and missing;
- recommended verification against the original or adjacent records.

For a sequence of pages, assign stable image identifiers and maintain a page-level log. Do not merge similarly named people across pages until identity evidence supports it.
